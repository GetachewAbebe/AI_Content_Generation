# Design

Status on 25 November 2026: story, audience and intent are written. **Nothing has been generated by a model yet.**

## Decided before generating (25 November 2026)
| Decision | Reason |
|---|---|
| Subject: a craft or skill passed on in the family | Chosen by the author |
| Narration in Amharic, English burned-in subtitles | The family's language; Amharic is on the Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS language list |
| Stylised, invented character and a stock voice | No real person is depicted or voiced |
| Vertical is the primary export | The author's audience watches on phones first, then desktops |
| Subject: the dawn coffee ritual, told through one fictional grandmother | One person, one room, one morning: easy to keep consistent, and the ritual is shared by the whole audience |

## Audience and intent
**Audience.** Ethiopian young adults and parents, in Addis Ababa and in the diaspora, watching on mobile phones (9:16) and desktops (16:9).

**Intent.** A quiet ache of gratitude and warmth: the love of home isn't loud, it lives in small daily rituals done before anyone else is awake.

**Why this matters to the author and family.** _To be written by the author._

## How the story works
- **Motif:** the cup. One lit window, then one pot, then more cups than people, then one cup beside a photo, then cups waiting in an empty room.
- **Light:** blue until shot 5, gold from shot 6. The brief had sunbeams in shot 5 and the sunrise in shot 6; shot 5 now has only first light, so the sun arrives once.
- **Voice:** a grown grandchild narrates; the last line is in Emama's own voice style.
- **Photo in shot 8:** angled away and out of focus, because generated faces in frames are unreliable and nobody real should be implied.
- **Ending:** the picture fades to black and the dark end card fades in. This is a fade through black, not a true dissolve.

Every shot's reason is in `storyboard.json` (`reason`, `why_backend`).

## Timing risk, estimated before any audio exists
99 Amharic words (the brief estimated 145). At an assumed 4.5, 5.5 and 6.5 fidel per second the film would run 104, 91 and 84 s, against a 90 s limit. The radio cut decides: measure first, then cut words or pauses.
